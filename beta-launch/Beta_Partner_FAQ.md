# ECHOES Beta: Frequently Asked Questions

Plain answers for museums, historical societies, archives, libraries, and educators.

---

**What exactly is ECHOES?**  
A source-grounded way for the public to converse with figures from California history. Visitors ask questions and get answers in period voice, drawn from curated public-domain sources and primary texts—not the open internet. Every reply is labeled by evidence, and citations are one tap away.

**What’s live today?**  
**California Speaks** — seven figures, roughly north to south: Mark Twain, Hubert Howe Bancroft, August Hemme, Myron Angel, Jesse D. Mason, Anita Loos, and Alonzo Horton. Try them at https://echoes-inky-zeta.vercel.app/

**Isn’t AI known for making things up?**  
Yes—and that’s the problem ECHOES is built to solve. Figures answer from a curated claim bank plus ingested primary texts for that person and place. Answers are labeled **Documented · Reasonable inference · Contested · Not in the sources**. When the record doesn’t support an answer, the figure says so instead of inventing biography.

**How accurate is it really?**  
It’s strong and improving, but not perfect—which is why we’re in **beta** and why **your expertise matters**. Feedback on accuracy, gaps, framing, and visitor usefulness is the most valuable thing a partner provides.

**What does it cost us?**  
During the beta there is **no licensing or subscription fee**. Light organizational trials are free to run. For high-volume public / exhibit use we can agree a simple **at-cost AI usage** arrangement (typically a few dollars a month) with an optional **monthly cap**—no markup, no surprises.

**Can you build a figure for our town, region, or exhibit?**  
Yes. During the beta I can build a **custom source-grounded figure** as a collaborative pilot—using public-domain materials you recommend and materials we research together. Ideal for a local chronicler, founder, gallery theme, walking tour, or classroom pilot. You help define the person/place and approve sources; I do the technical build.

**What do we have to do?**  
Very little: try it and tell us what you think. Optionally share source suggestions, photographs, topics, or ideas for a custom figure. No minimum time commitment; you can step back anytime.

**Do we need to install anything or involve IT?**  
No. ECHOES is a **web link and QR code**—nothing to install, no integration, no accounts for the public. Place a QR beside an exhibit, add a link to your site, or use it internally.

**How do we try it?**  
Open https://echoes-inky-zeta.vercel.app/ on any phone or computer. *(First question for a figure may take 30–60 seconds while sources load; after that it’s much faster.)* Matthew can also give a 15-minute demo in person, by phone, or video.

**Who owns our materials and content?**  
We use **only public-domain, your-own, or explicitly permissioned** material. You approve anything drawn from your collection before it goes live. You retain all your rights; the beta doesn’t transfer ownership.

**Is it appropriate for students and the public?**  
Yes. Designed for middle- and high-school readers and the general public, with careful handling of sensitive topics. Difficult history is treated with honesty and context.

**What about visitor privacy?**  
The public uses it **without creating accounts**. Data collection stays minimal; retention can be tailored to your organization’s needs.

**What’s the long-term vision?**  
Today: text + optional voice + matched historic images on the web. Next: richer classroom and gallery use, more regional figures. Destination: a **living portrait**—a physical exhibit experience where the figure comes to life and sends visitors back into the cases, maps, and photographs. Partners help choose what gets built and how it’s framed.

**Why are you doing this?**  
To make local and regional history more accessible and engaging, and to practice responsible, source-grounded AI for education and public interpretation—alongside the people who already safeguard these stories.

**What’s the catch?**  
There isn’t a hidden one. Try the beta, give honest feedback, leave whenever you like. In exchange, your expertise helps build something that serves real visitors and real places.
